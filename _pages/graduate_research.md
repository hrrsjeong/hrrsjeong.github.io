<article class="graduate-opportunity">
  <p class="graduate-opportunity-lead">
    The <strong>Computational Genomics &amp; Evolutionary Medicine Lab (PI: Hyeonsoo Jeong)</strong> is recruiting students for the integrated M.S.-Ph.D. and Ph.D. programs in the Department of Life Sciences at GIST.
  </p>

  <p class="graduate-opportunity-intro">
    We investigate how genomes and epigenomes evolve across primates, vary across human populations, and remodel within individual cells during aging and disease. Combining long-read genome assemblies and pangenomes, single-cell and 3D multi-omics, and statistical and AI modeling, we tackle genomic regions and cellular states that were previously inaccessible to conventional approaches.
  </p>

  <section class="graduate-opportunity-section" aria-labelledby="graduate-research-directions-title">
    <h2 id="graduate-research-directions-title">Thesis research directions</h2>

    <div class="graduate-opportunity-direction">
      <h3>1. Primate and human evolution</h3>
      <p>
        We compare high-quality primate genome assemblies and cell-type-resolved brain epigenomes across developmental stages to identify human-specific genes and regulatory changes, and investigate why evolutionary innovations in neuronal and glial regulation are enriched for neuropsychiatric disease risk.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Example topics:</strong> comparative ape genomes, cell-type-specific brain epigenome evolution, human-specific cis-regulatory elements, evolutionary links to schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental disorders
      </p>
      <p class="graduate-opportunity-papers">
        <em>Representative papers:</em>
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21917-7">Jeong et al., <em>Nature Communications</em>, 2021</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08816-3">Yoo et al., <em>Nature</em>, 2025</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07473-2">Makova et al., <em>Nature</em>, 2024</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1747-7">Mendizabal et al., <em>Genome Biology</em>, 2019</a>.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="graduate-opportunity-direction">
      <h3>2. Complex genome architecture and human diversity</h3>
      <p>
        Using long-read sequencing, telomere-to-telomere genome assemblies, and human pangenomes, we resolve segmental duplications and large structural variants that short-read sequencing cannot capture. We examine how these dynamic loci generate newly duplicated genes, vary across global and East Asian populations, and influence gene regulation and disease susceptibility.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Example topics:</strong> population-scale segmental duplications and structural variation, human pangenomes, emergence and functional divergence of duplicated genes, epigenomic variation across complex loci
      </p>
      <p class="graduate-opportunity-papers">
        <em>Representative papers:</em>
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02051-8">Jeong et al., <em>Nature Genetics</em>, 2025</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.01.052">Mao et al., <em>Cell</em>, 2024</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.79387">Jeong et al., <em>eLife</em>, 2022</a>.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="graduate-opportunity-direction">
      <h3>3. Cellular aging and disease</h3>
      <p>
        We integrate single-cell DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility, 3D genome conformation, and transcriptomes to investigate how accumulating epimutations erode epigenetic cell identity with age and how injured tissues enter failed repair states in chronic disease. We also examine cross-species conservation of aging trajectories and epigenomic remodeling during cellular reprogramming and rejuvenation.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Example topics:</strong> single-cell and 3D multi-omics of aging, somatic epimutation and cell identity erosion, maladaptive tissue repair in chronic kidney and brain disease, epigenomic remodeling during cellular rejuvenation
      </p>
      <p class="graduate-opportunity-papers">
        <em>Representative papers:</em>
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01221-z">Jeong et al., <em>Nature Aging</em>, 2026</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-024-01450-3">Jeong et al., <em>GeroScience</em>, 2025</a>.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="graduate-opportunity-direction">
      <h3>4. Computational methods and AI modeling</h3>
      <p>
        We develop computational methods and statistical models for complex structural variation across haplotype assemblies, sparse single-cell and 3D epigenome integration, and cellular state inference. We also apply and build machine learning and AI models that integrate multi-omic variation to study cell-type regulation, aging, and disease.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Example topics:</strong> analytical methods for repetitive genome regions, statistical modeling for single-cell and 3D epigenomes, multi-omic machine learning and AI modeling, reproducible open-source bioinformatics tools
      </p>
      <p class="graduate-opportunity-papers">
        <em>Representative papers:</em>
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01221-z">Jeong et al., <em>Nature Aging</em>, 2026</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02051-8">Jeong et al., <em>Nature Genetics</em>, 2025</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv1245">Jeong et al., <em>Nucleic Acids Research</em>, 2016</a>;
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-42227-5">Jeong et al., <em>Scientific Reports</em>, 2019</a>.
      </p>
    </div>

  </section>

  <section class="graduate-opportunity-section" aria-labelledby="graduate-training-title">
    <h2 id="graduate-training-title">Training and mentoring</h2>
    <p>Graduate students receive close mentoring and develop strong capabilities in:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Reproducible large-scale analysis in Linux and high-performance computing (HPC) environments</li>
      <li>Quantitative genomics and data science in Python and R</li>
      <li>Long-read genome assembly, pangenome, and structural variation analysis</li>
      <li>Single-cell and 3D multi-omics integration</li>
      <li>Statistical modeling, machine learning, and genomic AI development</li>
      <li>Scientific writing, international conference presentations, and open-source tool development</li>
    </ul>
    <p>
      Regular one-on-one and group meetings guide study design, biological interpretation, and manuscript preparation, supporting careers in academia, research institutes, and biotech/AI industry.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="graduate-opportunity-section" aria-labelledby="graduate-applicants-title">
    <h2 id="graduate-applicants-title">Who should apply?</h2>
    <p>We welcome students from two complementary backgrounds:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Life sciences, biotechnology, and biomedical majors</strong> who want to build rigorous computational and modeling skills to answer fundamental questions in evolution, diversity, aging, and disease</li>
      <li><strong>Computer science, statistics, mathematics, AI, and engineering majors</strong> who want to design new algorithms, statistical models, and AI frameworks for challenging genomic and single-cell datasets</li>
    </ul>
    <p>
      Prior programming or bioinformatics experience is helpful for biology majors, and prior biology coursework is helpful for quantitative majors, though neither is strictly required at entry. What matters most is intellectual curiosity and strong motivation to grow across both biology and computation.
    </p>
  </section>
