Graduate Research Opportunities

M.S.-Ph.D. integrated and Ph.D. opportunities in computational genomics and evolutionary medicine at GIST.

The Computational Genomics & Evolutionary Medicine Lab (PI: Hyeonsoo Jeong) is recruiting students for the integrated M.S.-Ph.D. and Ph.D. programs in the Department of Life Sciences at GIST.

We investigate how genomes and epigenomes evolve across primates, vary across human populations, and remodel within individual cells during aging and disease. Combining long-read genome assemblies and pangenomes, single-cell and 3D multi-omics, and statistical and AI modeling, we tackle genomic regions and cellular states that were previously inaccessible to conventional approaches.

Thesis research directions

1. Primate and human evolution

We compare high-quality primate genome assemblies and cell-type-resolved brain epigenomes across developmental stages to identify human-specific genes and regulatory changes, and investigate why evolutionary innovations in neuronal and glial regulation are enriched for neuropsychiatric disease risk.

Example topics: comparative ape genomes, cell-type-specific brain epigenome evolution, human-specific cis-regulatory elements, evolutionary links to schizophrenia and neurodevelopmental disorders

Representative papers: Jeong et al., Nature Communications, 2021; Yoo et al., Nature, 2025; Makova et al., Nature, 2024; Mendizabal et al., Genome Biology, 2019.

2. Complex genome architecture and human diversity

Using long-read sequencing, telomere-to-telomere genome assemblies, and human pangenomes, we resolve segmental duplications and large structural variants that short-read sequencing cannot capture. We examine how these dynamic loci generate newly duplicated genes, vary across global and East Asian populations, and influence gene regulation and disease susceptibility.

Example topics: population-scale segmental duplications and structural variation, human pangenomes, emergence and functional divergence of duplicated genes, epigenomic variation across complex loci

Representative papers: Jeong et al., Nature Genetics, 2025; Mao et al., Cell, 2024; Jeong et al., eLife, 2022.

3. Cellular aging and disease

We integrate single-cell DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility, 3D genome conformation, and transcriptomes to investigate how accumulating epimutations erode epigenetic cell identity with age and how injured tissues enter failed repair states in chronic disease. We also examine cross-species conservation of aging trajectories and epigenomic remodeling during cellular reprogramming and rejuvenation.

Example topics: single-cell and 3D multi-omics of aging, somatic epimutation and cell identity erosion, maladaptive tissue repair in chronic kidney and brain disease, epigenomic remodeling during cellular rejuvenation

Representative papers: Jeong et al., Nature Aging, 2026; Jeong et al., GeroScience, 2025.

4. Computational methods and AI modeling

We build algorithms and statistical models for sequence-resolved pangenome analysis, sparse single-cell and 3D epigenome integration, and cellular state inference. We also develop machine learning and genomic foundation model approaches that learn how DNA sequence variation shapes cell-type-specific epigenomic regulation and phenotypes.

Example topics: algorithms for repetitive genome regions, statistical modeling for single-cell and 3D epigenomes, deep learning and AI models of sequence-to-regulation grammar, reproducible open-source bioinformatics tools

Representative papers: Jeong et al., Nature Aging, 2026; Jeong et al., Nature Genetics, 2025; Jeong et al., Nucleic Acids Research, 2016; Jeong et al., Scientific Reports, 2019.

Training and mentoring

Graduate students receive close mentoring and develop strong capabilities in:

  • Reproducible large-scale analysis in Linux and high-performance computing (HPC) environments
  • Quantitative genomics and data science in Python and R
  • Long-read genome assembly, pangenome, and structural variation analysis
  • Single-cell and 3D multi-omics integration
  • Statistical modeling, machine learning, and genomic AI development
  • Scientific writing, international conference presentations, and open-source tool development

Regular one-on-one and group meetings guide study design, biological interpretation, and manuscript preparation, supporting careers in academia, research institutes, and biotech/AI industry.

Who should apply?

We welcome students from two complementary backgrounds:

  • Life sciences, biotechnology, and biomedical majors who want to build rigorous computational and modeling skills to answer fundamental questions in evolution, diversity, aging, and disease
  • Computer science, statistics, mathematics, AI, and engineering majors who want to design new algorithms, statistical models, and AI frameworks for challenging genomic and single-cell datasets

Prior programming or bioinformatics experience is helpful for biology majors, and prior biology coursework is helpful for quantitative majors, though neither is strictly required at entry. What matters most is intellectual curiosity and strong motivation to grow across both biology and computation.

How to apply

Please email the following materials prior to GIST graduate admissions:

  • CV
  • Academic transcript
  • A brief statement of research interests and motivation
  • Relevant coursework, research, programming, or data analysis experience
  • Intended enrollment term
  • Optionally, a GitHub link or examples of previous projects

After reviewing your materials, we will schedule an individual meeting to discuss research fit, potential thesis topics, and the GIST admissions process.
