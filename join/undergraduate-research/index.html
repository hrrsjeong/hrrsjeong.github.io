GIST Undergraduate Research Opportunities

Undergraduate research opportunities in computational genomics, epigenomics, and genomic AI at GIST.

The Computational Genomics & Evolutionary Medicine Lab (PI: Hyeonsoo Jeong) welcomes undergraduate students interested in hands-on research in computational genomics, epigenomics, and quantitative biology.

We study how genomes and epigenomes evolve across primates, diversify across human populations, and change across individual cells during aging and disease, combining long-read pangenomes, single-cell multi-omics, and statistical and AI modeling.

Research opportunities

Depending on background and interests, undergraduate researchers can participate in:

  • Comparative genomics and brain epigenome evolution across humans and primates
  • Population-scale pangenomes, segmental duplications, and structural variation
  • Single-cell and 3D epigenomic analysis of cellular aging, cell identity, and tissue repair
  • Statistical modeling and machine learning for multi-omic data integration
  • Development of reproducible bioinformatics workflows and visualization tools

Students who are newer to programming start by discussing key papers and learning Linux, Python or R, data visualization, and genomic data structures step by step.

Students with strong programming, statistics, or machine learning backgrounds can contribute directly to ongoing data analysis and method development projects, with opportunities for conference presentations and coauthorship based on substantive contributions.

Who should apply?

We welcome undergraduates from both biology/biomedical majors and computer science, AI, statistics, mathematics, or related majors who:

  • Are curious about human evolution, genetic diversity, cellular aging, or disease
  • Want hands-on experience analyzing real, large-scale sequencing and single-cell datasets
  • Enjoy connecting quantitative/computational thinking with biological questions
  • Can participate consistently over an agreed semester or vacation period

What you will learn

  • Linux and high-performance computing workflows
  • Quantitative data analysis and visualization in Python or R
  • Processing and biological interpretation of long-read genomic and single-cell epigenomic data
  • Statistical modeling and machine learning applications in genomics
  • Critical reading of scientific literature and clear research presentation

How to apply

Please email the following information:

  • A brief introduction and major/year
  • Research topics that interest you
  • Relevant coursework, coding, or research experience
  • Available start date and expected duration
  • Academic transcript

Optionally, you may include a GitHub link or previous coursework/project code. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis, followed by an individual meeting to discuss potential projects.
